## Results & Discussion Summary

The study yielded significant findings regarding student engagement with GenAI in higher education, characterized by generally positive attitudes tempered by specific reservations. Students recognized substantial utility in GenAI tools, citing benefits such as personalized, 24/7 support (Mean = 4.12, SD = 0.83), time savings (Mean = 4.20, SD = 0.82), and assistance in generating unique insights. This aligns with broader research suggesting GenAI can transform teaching and learning processes (Mizumoto and Eguchi, 2023).

A crucial finding was the strong positive relationship between student behavior and perception: willingness to use GenAI was significantly and positively correlated with both knowledge of the technology ($r = 0.189; p < 0.001$) and frequency of use ($r = 0.326; p < 0.001$). This suggests that increased exposure and understanding drive greater intended future adoption.

However, concerns remain salient. Students who rarely use GenAI showed significantly greater agreement that GenAI might generate factually inaccurate output (Mean = 3.99, SD = 0.847) compared to frequent users (Mean = 4.22, SD = 0.829), indicating that accuracy is a primary barrier for non-users. Furthermore, while students expressed moderate concern over issues like undermining educational value (Mean = 3.15) or hindering skill development (Mean = 3.10), these concerns did not significantly correlate with their overall knowledge of GenAI ($r = 0.096; p > 0.05$). The discussion emphasizes that student perceptions significantly influence learning approaches, echoing Biggs' (1999) framework, thus necessitating tailored integration strategies that address these specific concerns to maximize effective learning outcomes.
