## Results & Discussion Summary

The study yielded significant findings regarding student engagement and perception of GenAI in higher education. Overall, students demonstrated a positive inclination toward integrating GenAI tools, recognizing substantial potential benefits. Key perceived benefits include the ability of GenAI to save time (Mean = 4.20, SD = 0.82), its 24/7 availability (Mean = 4.12, SD = 0.83), and its utility in improving digital competence (Mean = 3.70, SD = 0.96). Furthermore, students strongly agreed that learning to use these technologies is crucial for future careers (Mean = 4.05, SD = 0.96).

A critical finding relates to the relationship between usage behavior and perception: students who use GenAI more frequently or possess greater knowledge are significantly more willing to integrate it into their future practices. This positive correlation suggests that familiarity drives acceptance. Conversely, concerns about factual inaccuracy were significantly higher among infrequent users (Mean = 3.99, SD = 0.847) compared to regular users (Mean = 4.22, SD = 0.829), indicating that exposure may mitigate skepticism regarding reliability. However, concerns about academic integrity (undermining education value, limiting social interaction, hindering skill development) generally registered lower mean scores (around 3.06 to 3.15), suggesting these are secondary worries compared to utility. The discussion highlights that, consistent with prior research (e.g., Mizumoto and Eguchi, 2023), GenAI offers efficiency gains, such as rapid grading and feedback. Ultimately, the results support the premise that student perceptions significantly shape learning approaches (Biggs, 1999), necessitating policy development that capitalizes on positive perceptions while actively addressing concerns like misinformation and over-reliance.
