## Academic Abstract

Generative Artificial Intelligence (AI), particularly Large Language Models (LLMs), is gaining significant traction in critical domains such as software safety engineering, where ensuring software reliability and preventing harm are paramount. This necessitates high-quality outputs from these AI systems, suggesting that simplistic reliance on raw LLMs is insufficient for meeting stringent safety requirements. This paper explores advanced methodologies required to effectively manage the complexities and inherent safety concerns within software systems when employing generative AI. The findings indicate that while LLMs can process structured data like XML, performance and accuracy improve when system descriptions align more closely with natural language. Furthermore, the study highlights the risks associated with equipping agents with multiple tools, emphasizing the need for linguistically distinct trigger conditions to prevent inaccurate tool invocation. Ultimately, the research underscores the necessity for sophisticated, guided approaches—such as structured Chain-of-Thought (CoT) flows—to ensure accurate task formulation and reliable information retrieval, while maintaining the crucial requirement that human oversight remains responsible for validating all AI-generated suggestions.
