## Detailed Methodology Analysis

The methodology employed in this study centered on a quantitative survey design targeting university students in Hong Kong to gauge their perceptions of Generative AI (GenAI) technologies, such as ChatGPT. The sample comprised 399 undergraduate and postgraduate students drawn from diverse academic disciplines, ensuring a broad representation of the student body's exposure and engagement levels.

The data collection instrument appears to have utilized Likert-scale items to measure agreement levels regarding specific statements related to GenAI integration, willingness to use, and associated concerns. For instance, willingness to use was assessed via statements like, "I envision integrating generative AI technologies like ChatGPT into my teaching and learning practices in the future" (Mean = 3.85, SD = 1.02) and concerns were measured using items such as, "Using generative AI technologies such as ChatGPT to complete assignments undermines the value of university education" (Mean = 3.15, SD = 1.17).

Statistical analysis involved descriptive statistics (Means and Standard Deviations) to summarize overall attitudes. Inferential statistics included independent samples t-tests to compare perceptions between different usage groups (e.g., those who never/rarely use GenAI versus those who use it sometimes). A key analytical component was correlation analysis (Pearson's $r$) to examine the relationships between variables, specifically linking perceived willingness to use GenAI with measures of GenAI knowledge and frequency of use. The results indicated significant positive correlations ($r = 0.189$ and $r = 0.326$, both $p < 0.001$), suggesting that greater familiarity and usage predict higher future adoption intent. The methodology successfully captured both the positive drivers and the underlying concerns influencing student adoption of these emerging technologies.
