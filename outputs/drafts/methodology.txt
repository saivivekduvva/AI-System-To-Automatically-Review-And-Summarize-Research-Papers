## Detailed Methodology Analysis

The methodology employed in this study centered on a quantitative survey design targeting university students in Hong Kong to assess their perceptions of Generative AI (GenAI) technologies like ChatGPT. A total sample size of 399 undergraduate and postgraduate students from diverse academic disciplines participated in the data collection. While the abstract confirms the use of a survey, specific technical details regarding the survey instrument's construction, validation, or the precise sampling frame (e.g., random sampling, convenience sampling) are not explicitly detailed in the provided text.

The analysis involved descriptive statistics (Means and Standard Deviations) to summarize student agreement levels on various statements related to willingness to use GenAI (Table 3) and concerns about GenAI (Table 4). Inferential statistics were robustly applied, including independent samples t-tests and correlation analysis. Specifically, t-tests were used to compare the perception of factual inaccuracy between students who never/rarely use GenAI versus those who use it sometimes, revealing significant differences ($t = 2.695, p < 0.01$). Furthermore, the study utilized Pearson correlation coefficients to examine relationships between variables: willingness to use GenAI was correlated against knowledge of GenAI ($r = 0.189; p < 0.001$) and frequency of use ($r = 0.326; p < 0.001$). The absence of a significant correlation between concerns and knowledge ($r = 0.096; p > 0.05$) was also noted.
