### Abstract

This study investigates university students' perceptions regarding the integration of Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) technologies, such as ChatGPT, within higher education settings. A quantitative survey was administered to 399 undergraduate and postgraduate students across diverse academic disciplines in Hong Kong to gauge their familiarity, willingness to engage, perceived benefits, and associated challenges. The findings reveal a generally positive attitude toward adopting GenAI for teaching and learning. Students highly value GenAI's potential for personalized learning support, assistance with writing and brainstorming, and enhanced research capabilities. Correlation analysis demonstrated that perceived willingness to use GenAI is positively linked to both knowledge of the technology ($r = 0.189; p < 0.001$) and frequency of use ($r = 0.326; p < 0.001$). Conversely, concerns regarding factual inaccuracy were significantly higher among infrequent users. These insights underscore the necessity for educators and policymakers to develop informed guidelines that address student concerns while strategically leveraging GenAI's capacity to enhance efficiency and learning outcomes.

### Methodology

This study employed a quantitative survey design to assess the perceptions of Generative AI (GenAI) technologies among university students in Hong Kong. The sample comprised 399 undergraduate and postgraduate students recruited from various academic disciplines. The survey instrument utilized a multi-item Likert scale structure to measure constructs including familiarity, willingness to use, perceived benefits, and associated challenges. Inferential statistics included independent samples t-tests and Pearson correlation analysis. Specifically, t-tests compared the perception of factual inaccuracy between infrequent and regular users. Pearson correlations examined the relationships between willingness to use GenAI, self-reported knowledge, and frequency of use.

### Results

Descriptive analysis indicated a generally positive inclination toward GenAI integration. Students recognized substantial utility, reporting high agreement with benefits such as time savings (Mean = 4.20, SD = 0.82), 24/7 availability (Mean = 4.12, SD = 0.83), and the importance of developing digital competence for future careers (Mean = 4.05, SD = 0.96). Concerns related to academic integrity (e.g., hindering skill development) registered lower mean scores (ranging from 3.06 to 3.15). Inferential statistics confirmed a significant positive relationship between behavioral engagement and acceptance: willingness to use GenAI was positively correlated with both knowledge ($r = 0.189; p < 0.001$) and frequency of use ($r = 0.326; p < 0.001$). Furthermore, a significant difference was found in concerns about factual inaccuracy between user groups ($t = 2.695, p < 0.01$), with infrequent users expressing significantly higher levels of concern (Mean = 3.99) regarding reliability compared to regular users (Mean = 4.22). The analysis did not find a significant correlation between concerns and knowledge ($r = 0.096; p > 0.05$).
