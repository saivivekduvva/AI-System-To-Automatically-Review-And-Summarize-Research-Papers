### Abstract

This study investigates university students' perceptions regarding the integration of Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) technologies, specifically tools like ChatGPT, within higher education settings. A quantitative survey administered to 399 undergraduate and postgraduate students across various disciplines in Hong Kong assessed familiarity, willingness to engage, perceived benefits, and associated challenges. The findings indicate a generally positive disposition towards utilizing GenAI in teaching and learning. Students identified significant potential in areas such as personalized learning support, assistance with writing and brainstorming, and enhanced research and analysis capabilities. Furthermore, correlation analysis revealed that perceived willingness to use GenAI is positively linked to both knowledge of the technology ($r = 0.189; p < 0.001$) and frequency of use ($r = 0.326; p < 0.001$). Conversely, concerns persist regarding factual inaccuracy and the potential undermining of educational value. These insights are crucial for educators and policymakers aiming to develop informed guidelines for the responsible and effective implementation of GenAI tools to enhance future learning experiences.

---

### Methodology

This study employed a quantitative survey design to gauge the perceptions of Generative AI (GenAI) technologies among university students in Hong Kong concerning their integration into higher education. The final sample comprised 399 undergraduate and postgraduate students drawn from diverse academic disciplines, ensuring broad representation of the student body.

Data collection utilized a structured questionnaire featuring Likert-scale items designed to measure agreement levels regarding specific statements related to GenAI integration, willingness to use, and associated concerns. For example, willingness to use was assessed using items such as, "I envision integrating generative AI technologies like ChatGPT into my teaching and learning practices in the future" (Mean = 3.85, SD = 1.02), while concerns were measured via items like, "Using generative AI technologies such as ChatGPT to complete assignments undermines the value of university education" (Mean = 3.15, SD = 1.17).

Statistical analysis commenced with descriptive statistics (Means and Standard Deviations) to summarize overall attitudes. Inferential statistics included independent samples t-tests to compare perceptions across different usage groups (e.g., infrequent versus frequent users). A primary analytical focus was Pearson's correlation ($r$) to examine the relationships between variables, specifically linking perceived willingness to use GenAI with measures of GenAI knowledge and frequency of use. ### Results

The analysis revealed generally positive student attitudes toward GenAI utility, tempered by specific reservations. Students recognized substantial benefits, citing personalized, 24/7 support (Mean = 4.12, SD = 0.83) and time savings (Mean = 4.20, SD = 0.82) as primary advantages.

A key finding involved the relationship between student behavior and perception: willingness to use GenAI was significantly and positively correlated with both knowledge of the technology ($r = 0.189; p < 0.001$) and frequency of use ($r = 0.326; p < 0.001$). This indicates that increased exposure and understanding predict higher intended future adoption.

However, concerns regarding accuracy remain salient. Students who rarely used GenAI showed significantly greater agreement that GenAI might generate factually inaccurate output (Mean = 3.99, SD = 0.847) compared to frequent users (Mean = 4.22, SD = 0.829). While students expressed moderate concern over issues like undermining educational value (Mean = 3.15) or hindering skill development (Mean = 3.10), these specific concerns did not significantly correlate with their overall knowledge of GenAI ($r = 0.096; p > 0.05$).
